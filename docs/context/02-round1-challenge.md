# SD Worx Round 1 case: "Unlock the Knowledge Within"

> **Find it. Understand it. Trust it.**
> How might we turn fragmented organisational knowledge into a trusted shared resource?

Note: the page with the SD Worx brief in the participants guide (qualifier, not published here) is an image. The text below comes from a participating team's summary in `de-vandepitjes/knowledge-trust/docs/challenge.md`.

## The problem in their words
- Knowledge lives in policies, manuals, procedures, checklists, emails, Teams, shared files, workflows, business apps and **in people's heads**.
- Finding is easy: search gives ten answers, an AI can summarise them. The hard part is **whether the answer is reliable, current and relevant for this particular customer, country or situation**.
- At its core this is a **trust problem**. A good solution shows not just an answer but *why it deserves trust*, where uncertainty remains and who can help.

## Their stories
1. Urgent customer question. The AI finds three documents: one recently updated, one without an owner, one that may apply to a different country. A colleague shares conflicting info in Teams. The employee has information but cannot act with confidence.
2. A payroll consultant inherits a client portfolio. The handover was one document and a one-hour meeting several years ago. The knowledge is spread across documents, chats, workflows, apps, business data and experts in different teams.

## What they asked for
- **Focused PoC.** Pick one meaningful problem. Don't solve everything.
- **One role, one flow, one knowledge source or one trust signal.**
- Make the **moment of doubt** concrete and show how the PoC takes someone from "I found something" to "I understand why I can trust it".
- Don't hide complexity behind a black box. Make trust **visible, explainable and usable**.
- Start from the friction, not from a technology.

## Areas of inspiration
| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Trust | How do you recognise whether info is relevant and reliable? |
| Capture | How does valuable knowledge become available outside inboxes, documents and silos? |
| Detect | How do conflicting, duplicated, missing or outdated knowledge become visible? |
| Connect | How do you find the right expertise when documents are not enough? |

## Likely tone for the final case
The same DNA is likely: real payroll-consultant friction, trust/control, explainability, human in the loop. All teams will have "trust receipt" ideas from Round 1. **Differentiate.**
