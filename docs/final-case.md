# SD Worx final case (verbatim, from the Finals Guide, 20 Oct 2026)

## ONE MORE CHALLENGE
**Can your solution stay trusted without becoming another task?**

You started with one challenge:
**How might we turn fragmented organisational knowledge into a trusted shared resource?**
Now take your solution one step further.

Knowledge changes. People change roles. Expertise evolves. Context gets lost.

How can your solution stay **useful, reliable and up to date** without asking people to constantly document, update or maintain it?

### Your extra challenge

**Keep it trusted. Make it transparent.**
Think invisible UI, calm technology and frictionless experiences:
- Automate what can reasonably happen in the background
- Verify when human judgement adds real value
- Minimise unnecessary effort and attention
- Integrate naturally into existing ways of working
- Make it understandable when people need to know, decide or act

The technology should be almost invisible when things work and clear when people need to understand or act.

**Think beyond documents**
Sometimes the most useful answer is not in a document. It is knowing:
- Who has dealt with this before?
- Who really knows this customer or topic?
- Who knows how this actually works here?
- Who knows why we do things this way?

**For example**
**You are a new developer at SD Worx.** You can look up how a build pipeline works. The harder questions are: **"How do we do this here?", "Who should I talk to?"**

**You need to launch a marketing campaign.** The harder questions might be: **"Who has done this before?", "Who understands this audience?", "Who knows what actually worked?"**

Could your solution learn from signals already created through everyday work, instead of requiring employees to maintain another knowledge system?
**These examples are inspiration, not requirements. Solve the underlying problem.**

### Show us how
Ask yourselves:
1. What happens automatically?
2. When is human verification genuinely valuable?
3. How do you prevent maintenance from becoming a burden?
4. How does your solution fit naturally into the user's workflow?
5. How does your solution remain understandable without demanding constant attention?

The goal is not zero human involvement. The goal is zero unnecessary effort.

**Find it. Understand it. Trust it. Keep it trusted. Make it transparent. Make it effortless.**

## SD Worx spoken briefing on the day (transcribed excerpt, lightly cleaned)

> ...it's transparent to the user, so trust it, but with minimal effort.
>
> Let me give you another example. When I started university, I got a pamphlet from the university with practicalities. Today it is probably an app, but then it was still a document. I also got a mentor. Why did I get that mentor? Because that mentor is telling me things that are not written down in documents. For instance, to be at that class at 4:00 on a Friday afternoon, because that professor will notice if you're not there and he will take points off your exam. Very important information.
>
> At SD Worx, we don't have one mentor; we have thousands of mentors, so we need a system with minimal effort. With technology, you need to tap into that knowledge. We don't mean zero human involvement, we mean zero unnecessary effort, so something that's easy to maintain.
>
> Another example: the fly on the wall. A fly can observe and see where the experience lives, and can also see who gets asked what. Mind you, that's an example. That's not the solution we want you to build.

**Takeaways for us:**
- The target is the **unwritten rules** a mentor passes on ("be there Friday at 4, the professor notices"), not just documents.
- "Thousands of mentors": the knowledge is spread over many people, so capturing it must cost each of them almost nothing.
- "See where the experience lives and who gets asked what" is a hint about the signal (who gets asked, who answers), but **not** the solution. Do not build pure passive surveillance of chats.
